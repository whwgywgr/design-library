The quiet return of the newspaper web

After a decade of chasing app-store gloss, a new generation of publications is rediscovering what print always knew: rules, columns and confidence.

By Aminah RosliSenior CorrespondentKuala Lumpur · 14 min read

The most radical thing a website can do in 2026 is look like it was typeset by someone who read a book. No infinite scroll, no pop-ups begging for an email address — just a masthead, a rule, and words that know where they belong.

The movement began quietly, in the archives. Designers started scanning broadsheets from the seventies and asking an uncomfortable question: why did we abandon the column? The answer, it turns out, was never typography. It was fear — fear that readers would leave, that whitespace was wasted, that a serif font read as "old".

“A page with rules and columns is a promise: someone decided what matters here.”

The economics are changing too. Newsletters taught publishers that audience is a relationship, not a metric. And a relationship, like a good column, has a fixed measure — it respects the reader's eye and their time.

Whether the newspaper web survives its second childhood is beside the point. For now, the drop caps are back, the rules are heavy, and reading on a screen feels, once again, like reading.
